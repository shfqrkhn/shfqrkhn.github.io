How the layers work

Foundation

The right turf or turf alternative reduces water use, fertilizer and mowing. A mix suited to your climate roots deeply and crowds out weeds from the ground up.

Border

A dense, low groundcover is one of the most effective ways to suppress weeds. This "green mulch" also holds soil moisture and replaces yearly wood mulch.

Structure

One key shrub acts as the garden's backbone. Pick one with interest in several seasons: flowers, foliage, fall colour and winter bark. It also gives birds shelter and food.

Hub

Three perennials that flower in sequence give pollinators food from spring to fall, including bees, butterflies and other beneficial insects.

Choosing native and well-adapted plants saves work and restores habitat for local wildlife. This sentence is the guide's own summary.

Next steps

General guidance, not sourcedAmounts and timing are general guidance. See Sources.

  1. Prepare the soil

    Before planting, mix a 2-4 inch layer of compost into the bed. Healthy soil is the base of a low-maintenance garden and cuts the need for fertilizer later.

  2. Plant and water

    While new plants establish, water deeply once or twice a week instead of sprinkling daily. This pushes roots down and makes plants more drought tolerant over time.

  3. Add natural mulch

    After planting shrubs and perennials, spread 2-3 inches of wood chips or shredded leaves around them, kept off the stems. Mulch holds moisture, suppresses weeds and feeds the soil as it breaks down.

Planting calendar sources

Frost dates: Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, "30-year Average Last Spring Frost (0 C)", 1991-2020 normal, file lt_lsf0_gs_1991_2020.tif (10 km grid, definition in the AAFC Data Product Specification, ISO 19131). Record: open.canada.ca dataset d316cd06-04ae-4275-917d-788ae75c1b2b. Files: agriculture.canada.ca file listing. Retrieved 30 October 2026. The values for the 56 places were read from the file by the guide author at the cell holding each place.

First fall frost: Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, "30-year Average First Fall Frost (-2 C)", file lt_fff-2_gs_1991_2020.tif (10 km grid; file dated 28 March 2024 in the AAFC file listing). Record: open.canada.ca dataset 858250ec-bc62-4889-90bc-ef8b337ec87d (licence field: Open Government Licence - Canada; record modified 22 April 2024). Files: agriculture.canada.ca file listing. Reference period of the file used: 1991-2020 (the record text states 1961-2010 and lists four periods; this guide uses the 1991-2020 file to match the spring dates). Retrieved 31 October 2026. Read at the same cell as the spring value for each of the 56 places; six British Columbia coast places have no value in the file.

Crop source by crop: snap bean, beets, broccoli, cabbage, carrots, cauliflower, collards, corn, cucumber, kale, kohlrabi and cantaloupe and melons all use the Penn State guide above. A Canadian provincial or federal planting table was sought on 31 October 2026; the Ontario Ministry of Agriculture page could not be read (access refused), and the other Canadian pages found are commercial or have no reuse terms, so none is used.

Crops: Penn State Extension, York County Master Gardeners, "Seed Planting Guide" (Vegetable-Seed-Planting-Guide.pdf), extension.psu.edu. Retrieved 30 October 2026. The crops in the calendar are taken from the first page of that table.

Place coordinates: Natural Earth populated places (public domain), retrieved 30 October 2026.
